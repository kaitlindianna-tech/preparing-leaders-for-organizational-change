## Project overview

Organizations often invest heavily in new technologies, restructuring, and strategic initiatives without adequately preparing the leaders responsible for helping employees navigate those changes. This white paper examines how leadership behaviors, individual characteristics, and developmental experiences can influence employee responses to organizational change. It translates findings from organizational psychology research into recommendations for building change leadership capability before major transitions occur.

The paper uses AI adoption as a timely example of a broader organizational challenge: introducing a change does not guarantee that employees will adopt new ways of working.

## Key questions

- How do employee reactions and change readiness affect implementation?
- Which leadership behaviors help employees navigate uncertainty and change?
- What leader characteristics and developmental experiences support effective change leadership?
- How can organizations assess, develop, and reinforce these capabilities over time?

## Research-informed insights

The paper examines three interconnected areas:

1. **Leadership behaviors:** Transformational leadership practices such as communicating a compelling vision, building trust, encouraging new thinking, and providing individualized support.
2. **Leader characteristics:** Positive self-concept and risk tolerance, including confidence, resilience, adaptability, and comfort with ambiguity.
3. **Developmental experiences:** Challenging assignments, practical application, feedback, and coaching that help leaders develop change leadership skills.

## Recommendations for organizations

The paper proposes an **Assess → Develop → Reinforce** approach:

| Stage | Recommended approach |
| --- | --- |
| **Assess** | Use 360-degree feedback and relevant validated assessments to understand current change leadership capabilities and development priorities. |
| **Develop** | Combine formal leadership training, opportunities to apply learning, progressively challenging assignments, and ongoing coaching. |
| **Reinforce** | Use pulse surveys, feedback, and development evidence to sustain effective behaviors and inform future leadership decisions. |

These are **research-based recommendations**, not outcomes from an implemented or experimentally evaluated intervention.
